Apollo’s lightweight panels put roof load in the spotlight

A company-materials briefing examines what a lightweight solar module changes—and why lower weight still leaves installation questions to answer.

Select an underlined term for a short explanation.

For a roof with limited load-bearing capacity, the first solar question may concern weight rather than available sunlight. Apollo Power, an Israeli developer of flexible solar technology, addresses that constraint with its Panda product family. This SPFI company briefing examines published technical materials prompted by an i24NEWS story supplied for review. It is an adaptation with explanatory context, not a manufacturer-authored release or an independent test.

In its AL1206 Panda F13 datasheet, Apollo lists a module weight of 5.8 kilograms, with a stated tolerance of 3%, and an area weight of 2.9 kilograms per square metre. These figures belong to that documented model. They should not be transferred to every Apollo product or described as the weight of a complete rooftop installation, which includes other components.

Lightweight packaging does not change the basic PhotovoltaicsConversion of light into electricity by solar cells. process: a cell absorbs light and produces electrical current. The US Department of Energy distinguishes conversion efficiency from the amount of electricity produced, which also depends on incoming light and cell performance. Weight and efficiency therefore answer different questions. A panel can be easier for a structure to carry without that fact alone establishing its annual electricity yield.

The practical limits appear in installation instructions. Apollo’s A12 AL1206 manual, available through Tadiran New Energy, describes attachment methods and substrate conditions, requires electrical design by certified personnel, and links screw placement to roof-bearing capacity and local weather. Although this is an older manual, it makes the underlying point clear: flexibility does not remove the need for a site-specific installation design and the applicable current documentation.

For readers comparing products, the useful next step is to ask which exact model and installation system a claim describes. The materials checked here do not provide an independent, comparable lifetime field study or a site-specific cost assessment. Apollo’s approach makes roof weight an explicit part of the discussion; the performance and suitability of an actual installation still need their own evidence.

One tender, many surfaces

Israeli councils have stopped buying solar one roof at a time. Framework tenders now bundle farms, reservoirs, cycle paths and batteries, and one shows what happens when nobody finishes the job.

Select an underlined term for a short explanation.

A few years ago a council bought a solar system for the school roof. In 2026 councils are increasingly buying a contractor for everything at once. The legal tool is a Framework tendera procurement that selects a supplier for future orders without committing to any specific project.: one competition picks a supplier, and each council later orders individual projects from it, with no obligation to order any.

The largest example SPFI found is the Coastal Plain municipal cluster. In July 2026 Golden Energy reported that its subsidiary Elgy Power had won the cluster’s framework as sole supplier for 16 local authorities, municipal companies, local planning committees and cooperatives. The scope reads like a catalogue of surfaces: AgriPV on farmland, floating PV on reservoirs, storage yards and solar roofs over cycle paths. The ceiling is up to NIS 1 billion over 36 months. The company estimates about 150 MW of potential. That is the winner’s estimate, not an order book.

The pattern repeats. Tamar Regional Council, by the Dead Sea, opened a framework for PV, storage and solar canopies in October 2026; the radar reports its deadline extended to 13 December. Kafr Qasim tendered “energy independence” projects with a corrected pack due 25 November. Savyon is buying a high-voltage battery facility, due 1 December. Emek HaYarden, Merom HaGalil and Sdot Dan have run similar combined energy tenders.

The most revealing document comes from Sdot Negev Regional Council. Its August 2026 tender seeks a contractor to take over rooftop PV systems that are already built. Some have never been connected to the grid, so they produce no electricity and no income. The contractor would inspect them, finish approvals, connect them and run them for five years, paid a share of revenue.

That is the risk inside every framework. Procurement is the easy part; permits, connection, operation and maintenance decide whether a roof produces anything. A council with one energy manager and twenty sites can sign a framework in a week and still have panels sitting idle years later.

SPFI’s questions for each framework are therefore practical. Which Call-off ordera specific project ordered under a framework. have been signed? Which sites are connected? Who reports output, and to whom?

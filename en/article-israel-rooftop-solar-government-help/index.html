A solar roof in Israel: what government help can do

The 2026 campaign points homeowners towards information, planning tools and regulatory guidance. Turning that help into a working roof still requires a project-specific decision.

Select an underlined term for a short explanation.

For a homeowner considering solar, the first useful government service may be a clearer route through the paperwork. On 26 July 2026, the Energy Ministry launched a campaign aimed at private houses and shared apartment buildings. It directed residents to a roof calculator and an information portal, while highlighting remuneration arrangements available through the end of 2026. That dated campaign is a starting point for checking the applicable rules, not a personalised promise of income.

The ministry’s website begins with a Feasibility assessmentAn initial check of whether a proposed project can work technically and economically at its particular site. of the particular roof before the household contacts an installer. This is the point to turn a broad ambition into a specific project. What can actually be installed, what would it cost, and what assumptions sit behind the expected income? Asking those questions gives the later commercial discussion a firmer foundation.

A separate tool, the ministry’s Mitkhadeshet portal, focuses on the regulatory journey. When inspected in September 2026, it was labelled as a pilot and covered rooftop systems up to 630 kilowatts. A short questionnaire leads towards a task list; the portal describes its purpose as gathering requirements, forms, costs and processing times. It also offers a way to report specific obstacles. This is administrative guidance, with the final requirements depending on the proposed installation.

Public support also includes rules that can reduce project costs. The State Comptroller’s June 2026 review describes planning and tax-related relief for rooftop solar, alongside continued financial and organisational barriers. These are different instruments from a universal cash grant. A TariffA defined rate used to calculate payment for electricity under a particular arrangement. determines payment under an applicable electricity arrangement; an exemption can remove a particular charge. Before treating either as household income, the owner needs the relevant conditions and a written project offer.

The most useful next step is to turn the guidance into questions for the project team: what equipment fits, which approvals remain, what costs recur, and who maintains the system? If electricity during an outage matters, ask specifically about Backup powerElectricity supplied to selected loads when the normal grid supply is unavailable, using equipment designed for that operation.: the official site describes combining storage with a hybrid inverter for that purpose. A calculator estimate can begin the discussion. A clearly specified, properly connected system is what eventually turns the roof into an electricity source.

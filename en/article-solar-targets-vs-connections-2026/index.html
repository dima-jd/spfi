Targets against delivery

Israel plans to triple its renewable capacity by 2035. In 2026 it is connecting less solar than in any year since 2020, while rooftop homes boom.

Select an underlined term for a short explanation.

Israel’s solar story in 2026 is two numbers moving in opposite directions.

The ambition. In May 2026 the Energy Ministry published a draft national strategy aiming for about 26 GW of renewable capacity by 2035, up from roughly 8 GW at the end of 2025. Almost all of it would be solar. At a November conference the ministry’s director-general framed the near-term goals as 18% renewable electricity by the end of 2026 and about 10 GW of storage by 2030, with more than NIS 40 billion for the grid.

The delivery. On 11 November Calcalist reported Electricity Authority data showing only 612 MW of solar connected in the first nine months of 2026, with the year expected to end below 850 MW. That would be the weakest year since 2020, after a record 2025. Connection requests rose to 7,246 MW, but 54% of the requested capacity was refused, reportedly because of Grid congestiona shortage of capacity on lines and substations to carry new generation.. The Authority blames mainly war-related delays to high-voltage projects. The ministry calls the grid “the main barrier”. Renewables supplied about 16% of electricity in 2025; industry figures now expect the 30% target to slip from 2030 to around 2032.

The exception is the home roof. Small rooftop systems connected about 134 MW in nine months, according to the same report. Ministry figures show almost 5,000 household systems connected in the first half of 2026, up 37% on the year before, and more than 40,000 homes now generating. Owners are rushing to lock in current tariffs before they close to new entrants at the end of 2026.

The cities in between. The State Comptroller’s June 2026 audit found that about 60% of local authorities’ solar potential is in built-up areas, yet only about 15% of it had been realised by the end of 2023. The audit lists the reasons: no municipal targets, slow grant payments (about NIS 39 million of NIS 248 million paid by June 2025), and 80% of authorities outside the ministry’s support programme. Only one municipal energy corporation had been approved.

The pattern suggests the bottleneck is no longer panels or public interest. It is wires, permits and municipal capacity. SPFI will track connections by segment each quarter against the 2035 path.

A tidal-energy forecast needs a scale—and a shoreline

MarketsandMarkets projects a larger market by 2031. The announcement offers a starting point for understanding a marine technology, not a measure of electricity already supplied.

Select an underlined term for a short explanation.

A market forecast can make a young energy technology sound as though its future has already arrived. In a 16 September 2026 release distributed by PR Newswire, MarketsandMarkets projected a tidal-energy market of US$198.5 million by 2031, from an estimated US$50.6 million in 2026. This SPFI adaptation treats those figures as the publisher’s forecast, not independently verified sales or installed generating capacity.

Tidal energyEnergy extracted from water movement associated with the tides. uses moving water rather than the light captured by a solar panel. The US Department of Energy places it within a broader marine-energy family that also includes waves and ocean currents. Turbines can extract energy from tidal currents; the same broad marine category contains devices with quite different operating principles. A market number therefore needs a clear definition of the equipment and activities being counted.

The US Energy Information Administration describes another approach: a barrage across a tidal basin controls water passing through turbines as water levels change. It also identifies possible effects on estuary plants and animals, water conditions and navigation. That physical setting matters. A renewable resource and a suitable engineering location are questions to examine together, rather than assuming every coastline offers the same opportunity.

The headline measures money, not electricity. Reading it as a forecast of megawatts installed or megawatt-hours delivered would change what it says. A rigorous comparison would ask which revenues are included, how the starting estimate was constructed and what assumptions drive the endpoint. SPFI inspected the public announcement, not the full commercial report or its underlying model, so the projection cannot be independently reproduced here.

For a solar-focused reader, tidal power is an international comparison in how renewable resources become infrastructure. A forecast describes a possible market; a project record describes an actual site and stage; operating measurements show what equipment delivers. Keeping those forms of evidence separate allows interest in the technology without converting a promotional projection into an accomplished energy transition.

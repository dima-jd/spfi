The land paper trail

Before a solar field appears in the news, it appears in a land record. Israel Land Authority notices from October and November 2026 show where projects are quietly moving.

Select an underlined term for a short explanation.

Most Israeli land is state land, managed by the Israel Land Authority (ILA). Before anyone can build on it, the ILA must grant a right: a short Use authorisationa limited right to use state land for a stated purpose., a lease, or a Development-to-lease agreementa deal under which a lease is granted once the developer completes the project. that turns into a lease once the project is built. Many of these are signed without a public tender, and the ILA publishes a notice of each one. Those notices are a quiet early signal of where solar is heading.

The rules behind the records. ILA policy lets an agricultural settlement build ground-mounted solar on up to 250 dunams of its land, and AgriPV on up to 500 dunams. Settlements on the “Confrontation linesettlements near Israel’s borders that receive special land and planning provisions.” near the borders get more: up to 400 dunams of ground solar and up to 1,500 dunams of AgriPV through joint ventures, until the end of 2028. A November 2025 ILA management decision proposed letting them convert unused ground-solar quota into AgriPV, one-for-one. It still needed the ILA Council’s approval.

The big records. The radar flagged two large development-to-lease agreements with Shikun & Binui Energy partnerships in the Eshkol region near Gaza. Tze’elim covers about 882,000 m² (882 dunams) under National Infrastructure Plan 51, with consideration of about NIS 33 million. Magen 1 covers about 180,000 m². A separate authorisation at Kibbutz Eilot in the Arava covers about 251,000 m² for AgriPV.

The small records. Between 1 and 19 November, notices appeared for Nir Israel, Rosh Hanikra, Nahalal, Hazorim, Olesh and Kfar Yehoshua. Most cover about 1,000 m²; Rosh Hanikra covers about 4,700 m². None says what will be built: a roof, a yard array, a field system or a farm structure.

A land right is not a permit, a grid connection or a construction start. The notices omit capacity, technology, developer plans and whether farming continues. Their value is as a map: matched to parcels, plans and satellite images, they show which villages are moving towards solar, and where large projects will claim open land, before any press release.

SPFI will treat each record as the first line of a project file, then look for the plan, the permit and the connection.

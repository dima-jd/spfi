Who does what in Israeli solar?

Enlight, Doral and Nofar illustrate the different jobs behind a solar project. A useful company guide starts with responsibilities rather than a league table.

Select an underlined term for a short explanation.

A company name on a solar site tells only part of its story. Who developed the project, who built it, who owns it, and who answers when production falls? Three companies with documented Israeli activity—Enlight, Doral and Nofar—offer a starting point for understanding these roles. They are selected as examples of overlapping activities, not as a ranking of size, service or investment quality.

Enlight presents its work as a chain running from development and engineering through finance, construction management and asset ownership. Its solar-project directory includes Israeli sites such as Halutziot and Orvim alongside projects abroad. That geography matters when reading a company portfolio: a global total does not describe Israel alone. A Project developerAn organisation that advances a project from an opportunity towards a buildable, financed installation. can connect many stages of a project without manufacturing the panels it installs.

Doral describes Israeli activities on commercial roofs, water reservoirs, fish ponds and land, together with storage. Its separate electricity-supply business adds another role: contracting to sell electricity to customers. The distinction helps explain why two companies discussing solar may be offering different things. One conversation concerns hosting a generating installation; another concerns purchasing electricity. The exact relationship belongs in the project or supply agreement.

Nofar's Israel page explicitly combines development, ownership, EPCEngineering, procurement and construction: designing a system, obtaining its equipment and building it. and Operations and maintenanceThe monitoring, servicing and repairs needed during a system's operating life.. It lists rooftop, reservoir and land-based systems as well as storage. EPC covers engineering, procurement and construction; O&M concerns the operating years that follow. The page also groups some capacity figures as connected and ready to connect. That wording is a reason to check status before comparing portfolios, rather than counting every listed megawatt as already producing.

For a roof owner or local organisation, the practical next step is to map the offer onto those responsibilities. Who is the contracting party, who maintains the equipment, and which obligations survive a change of owner? For readers following the industry, dated project records and consistent categories are more informative than an unexplained top-ten list. A good company profile makes the work and accountability visible before it compares scale.

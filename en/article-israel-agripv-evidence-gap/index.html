Israel’s AgriPV evidence gap

Israel is writing AgriPV rules for thousands of dunams. The public, measured evidence for how Israeli crops behave under panels still fits on a few pages.

Select an underlined term for a short explanation.

The new AgriPV procedure will judge farms against yield norms (see “AgriPV gets a referee”). That raises a simple question: what do we actually know about Israeli crops under Israeli panels?

The clearest result is about tomatoes. In 2022 and 2023, researchers at Bar-Ilan University grew Processing tomatotomatoes grown for paste and sauce, harvested by machine. in seven rows between two PV arrays and measured what happened at different distances from the panels. The study was published in Scientific Reports in December 2025. According to Tomato News’s summary, rows in heavy shade directly under the panels yielded 40–55% less than open field. Moderately shaded rows lost about 10–20%, and rows in near-full sun lost little or nothing. Sugar content stayed mostly stable, but Dry matterthe solid share of fruit after water is removed. fell in heavy shade, which matters to paste processors. Shaded plants also had weaker roots and more powdery mildew. With electricity income counted, revenue per area was nine to ten times that of tomatoes alone.

That combination is the honest picture: AgriPV can make a field far more profitable while making part of it agriculturally worse. Under the draft rules, the bottom rows could decide a project’s tariff.

The largest programme has not published results. Israel’s national pilot was announced as about 120 AgriPV pilot plots with roughly $5 million of government support. The radar cites an Israel–Germany workshop summary describing nine technologies across those plots. SPFI found no public site list, protocol or interim dataset.

Greenhouses offer a different route. The EU-funded REGACE project hangs PV inside existing greenhouses. An August 2025 summary by Tel Aviv University’s Liron Amdur reports about 48 kg of steel per kW in the research greenhouses versus about 103 kg/kW in Israeli open-field AgriPV, and investment of about €921/kW versus €1,200/kW. A model farm at Zemer has two 500 m² greenhouses of tomatoes and cucumbers under monitored PV since July 2025. No yield figures have been published yet.

The gap matters because regulation is moving faster than evidence. Crop-specific results should come before crop-specific rules. SPFI would like to see the national pilot publish its plots, crops, geometry and raw results, including the combinations that failed.

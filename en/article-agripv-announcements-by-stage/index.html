Energized is not operating

Israel’s biggest AgriPV announcements of the autumn are planning permissions and tender wins. Here is what each one actually allows, and what it doesn’t.

Select an underlined term for a short explanation.

Autumn 2026 brought a run of large announcements. Read quickly, they suggest hundreds of megawatts of farm solar are arriving. Read by stage, they show something narrower: a few documents that let companies start the long route to a working site.

Hadarei She’an, Valley of Springs. Doral’s existing ground-mounted Stage A, about 175 MW of solar with 722 MWh of storage, has been electrically Energizedconnected and carrying current for the first time.. That is a physical milestone, not a commercial one: Commercial operation (COD)the date a plant formally starts selling power under its contracts. is expected “in the coming months”. On 12 October Doral reported government authorisation to advance Stage B as AgriPV: about 150 MW and 800 MWh across roughly 1,700 dunams belonging to 11 kibbutzim. Bizportal stresses this is a planning step, not approval to build. Doral has not disclosed the investment or schedule. Its NIS 150 million annual revenue figure is a company forecast.

Mikveh Israel, Holon. On 6 October Doral reported winning a tender at Mikveh Israel, Israel’s first agricultural school, founded in 1870. The project is about 50 MW with 300 MWh of storage on some 500 dunams, a few kilometres from Tel Aviv. No crop plan, investment, schedule or planning route has been published. It would be one of the first large AgriPV sites in the central district, in one of the country’s most historic farm landscapes.

The border schemes. On 11 November the cabinet authorised PowerGen to plan an AgriPV farm on land of kibbutzim Gesher HaZiv and Eilon near Nahariya, and Nofar Energy one at Moshav Shokeda near Gaza. Globes reports the sites will have no perimeter fences. Both now go to the National Infrastructure Committeethe fast-track planning body for nationally important projects.; Globes expects grid connection by the early 2030s if they are approved.

Why insist on the difference? Because each stage carries different risk. A planning authorisation can be rejected or reshaped. A tender win can stall at permitting. An energized plant can still fail acceptance tests or face curtailment. Reporting all of them as “new solar” inflates the pipeline and hides where projects actually get stuck.

SPFI will track these four sites through the same stages: plan deposited, plan approved, financial close, construction, energized, commercial operation and, for the AgriPV parts, measured farming under the panels.

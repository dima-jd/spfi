100,000 solar roofs: the programme behind the number

A residential programme aims to add many small electricity producers. Its launch, intended tools and later publications tell us what the government is trying to achieve—and what still needs measuring.

Select an underlined term for a short explanation.

A national electricity programme can begin with a very local decision: whether a residential roof should also produce power. On 18 February 2025, Israel’s Energy Ministry launched its 100,000 solar roofs programme for private houses and shared buildings. The aim was 100,000 additional residential solar roofs by 2030. That is a target for new installations, not a statement that those systems already exist.

The launch identified several tools: proposed tariff arrangements, tax-related changes, simpler connection procedures and public information. These were presented as parts of the programme, with different stages of decision-making. A TariffA specified rate for calculating payment for electricity under an applicable arrangement. concerns payment for electricity; a connection procedure concerns permission and technical steps. Putting both under the word support can obscure the practical difference. One affects the financial calculation, while the other affects whether a project can proceed.

The programme continued beyond its launch announcement. On 16 February 2026, the ministry published a report page again identifying the 100,000 additional-roof objective for 2030. In July 2026, a separate campaign directed residents towards information and calculation tools. This sequence shows a programme being communicated and developed over time. It does not, on its own, provide a verified count of completed installations or establish which announced tools apply to a particular building.

A roof count also leaves an important question unanswered: how much electricity can those roofs produce? Installed capacityThe rated power of installed generating equipment. and Electricity outputThe amount of electrical energy a system produces during a stated period. answer different questions. Imagine two groups containing the same number of roofs, but with different system sizes. The counts match, yet the generating equipment need not match. Actual output adds another measurement. This is an illustrative comparison, not a forecast of the programme’s results.

For a household or building committee, the programme is best read as a framework around a specific decision. What does the proposed system include, who will own it, what remains to be approved, and which costs and responsibilities continue after installation? For national progress, the matching questions are how many additional systems connect, what capacity they add and what they generate. Those records would turn the headline number into a picture of working residential solar.
